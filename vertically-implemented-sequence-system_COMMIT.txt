## The Problem

Your truck's loading rule is: **the stuff you unload LAST goes at the very back,
and the stuff you unload FIRST sits closest to the door.**

- Seq 8 = unload last → deepest, at the back wall
- Seq 7 → a bit closer to the door
- ...
- Seq 1 = unload first → right at the door

Before my fix, the computer **wasn't really enforcing this**.
It treated the sequence like a gentle suggestion, not a rule. 
So at the back of the truck you'd get a jumbled mix of sequence 7, 8, and 5 stacked together,
and items of the *same* sequence got split into two separate piles. Bad for the unloading crew.

## The New Way: "Rooms" in the Truck

Imagine the truck divided into **8 imaginary rooms in a line** — like slicing a loaf of bread:

```
┌──────────────────────────────┐
│ Door  [1][2][3][4][5][6][7][8]  Back wall
└──────────────────────────────┘
```

- Room 8 = all the way at the back (unload last)
- Room 1 = at the door (unload first)

**Each sequence gets its own room.** The room's size is based on 
how much stuff that sequence has (more stuff = bigger room, but always in the right order from back to door).

Then the computer packs:
1. Seq 8's boxes **only inside room 8** (the back room)
2. Then Seq 7's boxes **only inside room 7**
3. And so on toward the door

If a room is too small, the leftover boxes spill *forward* 
(toward the door, unloaded sooner — never backward into another sequence's room).

## The Two Technical Bugs I Also Had to Squash

**Bug 1: "The computer placed the boxes but forgot to write it down."**
The packing engine would put a box in the right spot, say "all done!",
but never added it to its official "items placed" list. So the app reported *0 boxes placed, 7 boxes left out* even though the layout was correct — like a worker who moved crates but forgot to tick them off the checklist.

**Fix:** I made it tick every box off the list the moment it's placed 
(and also add its weight to the truck load total, same as the normal path does).

**Bug 2: "The measuring tape disagreed with itself by half a hair."**
The room boundaries were crazy-long decimals (like `200.29806259314458`),
and the engine's tolerance was so tiny it rejected places that were off by 
less than the width of a human hair — so boxes bounced out of perfectly good spots.

**Fix:** Room boundaries are now rounded to a clean 3 decimals,
and the tolerance was loosened to half a step of that rounding, so "good enough" placements are accepted.
